**Submission of a review article: "Closed-loop cold atmospheric plasma delivery with embedded sensing: a critical review of sensing, control and integration, with an in-silico analysis of where dose error originates"**

I am submitting the above manuscript for consideration as a Review Article in *Biosensors and Bioelectronics*.

Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) is a growing redox-based therapy for chronic wounds and oncology. Its clinical reproducibility is limited because it is delivered in open loop, so the dose a patient receives depends on tissue, geometry and ambient conditions that the device does not measure. Existing reviews treat CAP dosimetry, plasma control and wearable CAP hardware separately. This manuscript brings the four layers of a closed-loop system (sensing, state estimation, control and actuation) together and grades the maturity of each against common evidence criteria.

The review reaches one main conclusion that matters for this journal's readers: the bottleneck is the sensor, not the controller. Constrained model predictive control already holds CAP thermal dose within safety limits in the laboratory. Electrochemical sensors for reactive species, by contrast, drift, foul and lose selectivity under plasma exposure. A reproducible Monte Carlo case study (code supplied as Supplementary Material S1) puts numbers on this. Feedback cuts the median thermal-dose error from 89% to 17%, but the remaining error follows sensor bias and lag. We also propose six benchmark metrics and a staged, metric-gated roadmap that biosensor developers can use directly.

The manuscript is a critical narrative review, not a systematic review. Every reference was checked against its DOI and against the specific claim it supports. The simulation is illustrative and is labelled as such throughout.
